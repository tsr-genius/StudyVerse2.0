Motion — Core ideas

Physics · Revision

1. Definition and key vocabulary

Motion is a change in position over time. Distance is the total path travelled; displacement is the straight-line change in position with direction. Speed is distance per unit time. Velocity is speed in a stated direction. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.

2. Formula triangle and units

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time. Rearranging gives distance = speed × time and time = distance ÷ speed. Use distance in metres (m), time in seconds (s), speed in metres per second (m/s). Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time, measured in m/s².

3. Worked example — every step

Question: A cyclist travels 240 m in 30 s. Calculate the average speed.

Step 1 — identify the quantities: distance = 240 m; time = 30 s; speed = ?

Step 2 — choose the equation: speed = distance ÷ time, because distance and time are known.

Step 3 — substitute: speed = 240 m ÷ 30 s.

Step 4 — calculate: 240 ÷ 30 = 8.

Step 5 — include units: average speed = 8 m/s.

Step 6 — check: 8 m/s × 30 s = 240 m, which matches the original distance.

4. Graphs and diagrams

On a distance–time graph, a steeper line means greater speed; a horizontal line means the object is stationary. On a velocity–time graph, gradient represents acceleration and area under the graph represents displacement (for a velocity graph with signed values).

Distance–time sketch: rising straight line = constant speed; flat line = stopped; changing gradient = changing speed.

5. Questions to answer

  1. A bus travels 360 m in 45 s. Calculate its average speed and show all working.
  2. A runner moves at 4 m/s for 25 s. Calculate the distance travelled.
  3. A journey of 150 m takes 30 s. Calculate the average speed in m/s.
  4. What does a horizontal section of a distance–time graph mean? Explain.
  5. Explain the difference between distance and displacement.
Suggested answers

1. 360 ÷ 45 = 8 m/s. 2. 4 × 25 = 100 m. 3. 150 ÷ 30 = 5 m/s. 4. The object is stationary because distance does not change with time. 5. Distance is total path length; displacement is straight-line change in position with direction.

6. Exam tips and common mistakes

  • Write the equation before substituting numbers.
  • Convert units first if the question mixes km, m, minutes or seconds.
  • Always include the unit in the final answer.
  • Do not confuse distance–time graph gradient with velocity–time graph gradient.
  • Average speed uses total distance divided by total time, including any stops unless the question says otherwise.
